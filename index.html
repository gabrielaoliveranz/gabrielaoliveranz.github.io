Terroir

Where should kiwifruit actually grow in the Bay of Plenty?

Geospatial land-suitability analysis

Four live New Zealand government datasets — property boundaries, soil surveys, land-cover maps and climate records — are lined up against each other and used to score every rural parcel across four Bay of Plenty districts for how well suited it is to growing kiwifruit.

What I built

Source→ Validate→ Spatially join→ Score→ Visualise

While double-checking the results, I caught something that couldn’t be true: a smaller, more exclusive group of high-scoring land had more parcels in it than the larger group it was supposed to fit inside. That’s mathematically impossible, and tracing it back showed the scoring logic had been quietly filing 21.7% of parcels into the wrong tier. I fixed it, and every step of that fix is in the public commit history.

The bug was more valuable than the finished chart.

What this demonstrates

  • Four different government datasets, each with its own format and quirks, combined into one picture instead of just assumed to line up.
  • A scoring model where every rule and cut-off is written into the code itself — not buried in a spreadsheet that only I could explain.
  • A bug caught by testing a simple rule of logic (a smaller group can't be bigger than the group it came from), not by squinting at a map and hoping something looked off.
  • Every parcel's score can be traced back to exactly which inputs and rule produced it — nothing is a black box.
  • A dashboard built for someone who needs an answer this week, not a wall of technical documentation.

Apophenia

What happens when an export season starts going wrong?

Operational risk intelligence simulator

    with a fixed-width projection band
  • 4

Nine live operational variables — weather, port congestion, fruit quality and regulatory load among them — drive a simulator that recalculates risk, cost of delay and a 26-week forecast in real time, against four independent public APIs.

What happens under the hood

Weather + port congestion + fruit quality + regulatory load→ Risk model→ Cost of delay + forecast→ Interactive scenario

Built with hand-written JavaScript, Chart.js and Mapbox GL JS.

Why I built it

A static report shows what already happened. This simulator lets someone change one input — a closed port, a quality downgrade — and see the operational and financial consequence before it happens for real.

A simulator lets you ask: “What happens if this changes?”
