Terroir

Where should kiwifruit actually grow in the Bay of Plenty?

Geospatial land-suitability analysis

Four live government and public datasets — LINZ cadastral parcels, S-map soils, LCDB land cover and Open-Meteo climate — feed a Python pipeline that joins them spatially and scores every rural parcel across four Bay of Plenty districts for kiwifruit suitability.

What I built

Source→ Validate→ Spatially join→ Score→ Visualise

While validating the results, I found a bug that had misclassified 19.4% of parcels — caught because a filtered subset came out larger than the group it was filtered from, which is impossible. Every fix like that lives in the public commit history.

The bug was more valuable than the finished chart.

What this demonstrates

  • Four government datasets with different schemas, coordinate systems and update cycles, reconciled into one spatial join rather than assumed compatible.
  • A scoring model with its weights and thresholds stated in the code, not buried in a spreadsheet only I can read.
  • A logic bug caught by checking an invariant — a filtered subset can't be larger than its source — not by scanning the map for something that looked wrong.
  • Every parcel's suitability score traceable back to the exact inputs and rule that produced it.
  • A dashboard built for someone who needs an answer this week, not a data dictionary.

Apophenia

What happens when an export season starts going wrong?

Operational risk intelligence simulator

  • 9
    Live variables
  • 26wk
    Forecast
    with a fixed-width projection band
  • 4
    Data feeds
    each independently guarded

Nine live operational variables — weather, port congestion, fruit quality and regulatory load among them — drive a simulator that recalculates risk, cost of delay and a 26-week forecast in real time, against four independent public APIs.

What happens under the hood

Weather + port congestion + fruit quality + regulatory load→ Risk model→ Cost of delay + forecast→ Interactive scenario

Built with hand-written JavaScript, Chart.js and Mapbox GL JS.

Why I built it

A static report shows what already happened. This simulator lets someone change one input — a closed port, a quality downgrade — and see the operational and financial consequence before it happens for real.

A simulator lets you ask: “What happens if this changes?”
