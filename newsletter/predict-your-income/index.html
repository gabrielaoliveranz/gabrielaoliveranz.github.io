Cash flow

Can you say what next month's income will be?

Kia ora,

Most small business owners can tell me last month's sales. Far fewer can say, with any confidence, what next month will look like. That gap is where cash flow stress starts.

Cash flow pressure on New Zealand small businesses

  • 29%Used a personal credit card for business expenses
  • 74%Say credit is important for managing cash flow
  • 63%Name customer retention as a key concern

Source: Visa New Zealand, research by Talbot Mills Research (online survey of 511 New Zealand businesses with 50 or fewer employees, 4 to 12 July 2025, margin of error ±4.3%). The 63% is from a prompted list of concerns.

Credit can bridge a gap. It can't tell you whether a gap is coming. A simple forecast can, and it takes less time than most people expect.

A cash flow forecast is an estimate of the money coming in and going out of your business over a future period. It can live in a spreadsheet. Two habits make it useful.

Check 1Look back before you look forward

Business.govt.nz's advice for established businesses is to use your past financial data to predict future income, then add anything you expect to change: a busy season, a marketing push, a holiday period.

Example (invented numbers)

What the last 12 months show
A plumber's income dips in January and July, every year
What that lets you do
Plan for those months in October and April, instead of finding out in the bank account

The pattern was always there. It only becomes useful once someone writes the twelve months down side by side.

Check 2Plan for three months, not one

Business.govt.nz suggests three versions of your income: a pessimistic estimate, a realistic one and an optimistic one. It also names being too optimistic as a common mistake, so the pessimistic one is the one to look at first.

Example (invented numbers): next month's income against $10,500 of bills

Pessimistic
$9,000
Realistic
$12,000
Optimistic
$15,000
Gap in the pessimistic case
$1,500

A $1,500 gap you know about a month ahead is a phone call to a customer who owes you, or a chat with your bank. The same gap discovered on the day is a personal credit card.

If you can still pay your bills in the pessimistic case, you're in good shape. If you can't, you now have a month to do something about it.

Try this · 15 minutes

  1. List your income for each of the last 12 monthsYour accounting software or bank statements will do. One column is enough.
  2. Circle the two weakest months and note whyHolidays, weather, a lost customer, a late payer. The reason matters as much as the number.
  3. Write next month's income three waysPessimistic, realistic, optimistic. Then put your bills against the pessimistic one.

Redo it once a month. The second time takes five minutes.

Worth knowing

  • A forecast can also help with timing. Business.govt.nz lists working out the best time to invoice and planning for future cash shortfalls among its uses. Cash flow forecasting
  • New businesses can still forecast. With no history yet, Business.govt.nz points to Stats NZ's Annual Enterprise Survey and Inland Revenue's industry benchmark figures. Cash flow forecasting

Figures: Visa New Zealand (research by Talbot Mills Research, July 2025). Guidance: Business.govt.nz, Cash flow forecasting (reviewed July 2025). General information, not financial advice. Check anything specific to your business with your accountant.
